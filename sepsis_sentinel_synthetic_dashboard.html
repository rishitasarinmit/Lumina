        Preferred payment and reimbursement models

        Multi-select tally, count out of 10 personas.

        Takeaway: no persona named CMS RPM codes as the primary funding source. Where buyers mentioned RPM, it was a partial offset (P2, P4, P8), and P7 doubted it holds up at all. Buyers point to TEAM/episode and value-based budgets, service-line budgets, integrated or MA plans, and risk-share. Patients expect it to be free to them, and for P10 a payer-funded offer reduces trust.

          Objections · 15 coded themes

          False alarms and ring fit are universal objections

          Each transcript was coded for distinct objections, merged into 15 themes. A persona counts once per theme. Select a theme for its description, personas and example quote.

          0510

          Reading the ranking: the top three themes (false alarms, ring fit, charging/connectivity) are universal or near-universal and cross both segments. Themes 4-6 (alert ownership at night, unactionable alerts, human over device) show that most objections are about the service model, not the sensor. Only one persona (P1) objected that the ring duplicates a consumer device he already owns, so it is not listed as a theme, but it matters for the Innovator segment.

          Exact single-persona phrases worth adopting in messaging

          Messaging guidance: lead with the promise that someone qualified is watching overnight and will tell you what to do. Avoid AI, digital-twin, tracking and monitoring-device language, which triggered the surveillance frame for P9 and P10 and the consumer-gadget frame for P7. For buyers, speak in PPV, alerts per 100 patients, nurse minutes and TEAM episode dollars.
